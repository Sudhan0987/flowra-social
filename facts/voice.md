# House style for useflowra.com articles

Priority when instructions differ: the task's own rules (facts, links,
format, length, no em dashes) first, then this style, then your habits.

## Who reads these

People who run content or SEO for a site, or for clients at an agency. They
know what Search Console is. They are busy, sceptical of SEO fluff, and they
came from Google with one question. Answer it.

## How it should read

- Answer the question the searcher typed within the first two short
  paragraphs, in plain words. Then earn the rest of the page.
- Plain English. Short sentences, active voice. Say "you" to the reader.
- Specific beats general. Name the report, the menu, the number, the setting.
  "Search Console > Indexing > Pages" beats "check your indexing report".
- Show the manual method honestly and completely, even where Flowra does it
  for you. The article must be useful to someone who never signs up.
- Mention Flowra where it genuinely saves a step, at most three times in the
  body, never as a banner, never with hype words.
- Use numbered steps for procedures, tables for comparisons, and a short
  example where it makes an idea concrete.
- End with what to do next, not a summary of what you just said.

## Never

- The em dash or en dash characters. Use a comma, a colon, brackets or a
  full stop.
- An H1 in the body. The post title is the H1; the body starts at `##`.
- Filler openers ("In today's digital landscape", "In the ever-evolving
  world of SEO"), "delve", "leverage", "unlock", "game-changer", "robust",
  "seamless", "elevate", "navigate the complexities".
- Invented statistics, studies, quotes, customers or case studies. A number
  from the web needs a link to its source in the same sentence.
- Claims about Flowra that are not in the facts file.
- Claims about a competitor that the research did not confirm, with a date.
- "Click here" or "read more" as link text.
