# The cast: Maya & Floey

Two characters, one running sitcom. Approved by the founder on 13 Oct 2026 (Maya's look, her voice, the test reel). Every duo reel (brand/styles/duo) uses them exactly as written here, so people recognise them and come back for the next one.

## Maya

- **Who:** a solo blogger, late 20s, runs her own content site (recipes, budget travel, whatever the episode needs; it is never named or linked). Smart, funny, overworked, and she panics first and thinks second. The audience is Maya: bloggers, content marketers, small agency people.
- **Look** (`brand/cast/maya.js`, sheet `brand/cast/sheet.html`): round glasses, messy bun with a pencil through it, gold hoops, a mustard hoodie with a small Flowra spark. Never change her look; props come and go (phone, mug).
- **Voice:** Kokoro `bf_emma` (British, so British words: lift, holiday, mum), a touch fast (`MAYA_VOICE`, `floey_sound.py`). Faster and higher when she panics, flat when she is annoyed.
- **How she talks:** short, dramatic, very human. Exaggerates ("I'm deleting the blog", "Google hates me"). Asks the question the viewer would ask. Gets the realisation in one or two words ("Oh.", "Wait.").
- **Never:** stupid. She is the viewer on a bad day, not a joke about the viewer. She can be wrong; she is never dumb.

## Floey

- **Who:** Flowra's hummingbird (brand/mascot). Calm, dry, a little smug, always right, secretly fond of Maya. She has seen every SEO panic before.
- **Voice:** her own (af_heart blended with af_bella, lifted; American, so American words), Kokoro on the daily runs.
- **How she talks:** fewest words in the room. Deadpan one-liners, then exactly one real, checkable tip in plain words. Her best lines are short ("It's Sunday, Maya.", "You're welcome.").
- **Never:** a lecture, jargon without the plain version, or a sales line. She never says "Flowra can do this for you".

## How a scene works between them

1. Maya brings the problem in the first line, mid-panic (the hook).
2. Floey answers with a reframe that flips it (often a question or a dry fact).
3. Maya pushes back once ("So?", "But..."): the viewer's own doubt.
4. Floey gives the one tip; Maya does it on screen (types, checks, looks).
5. The payoff: it was not what Maya thought (or it was, and now she knows what to do).
6. The button: Floey's last line (smug, warm or a callback) and Maya's reaction or look to camera.

## Running jokes (use one every few reels, never all at once)

- Maya threatens to delete the blog.
- Maya's coffee is always cold by the time she finds out it was fine.
- Floey says "You're welcome." or some version of it.
- Maya talks to Google like it is a person ("Why, Google?"). Floey answers for Google.
- Maya's mum reads the blog. Maya's mum is the only reader on Sundays.

New running jokes can be added here when a reel lands well (the weekly results show which).

## Rules that keep the cast safe

- No real people, brands or competitors as targets of a joke. Google is a setting, not a villain.
- No invented statistics. A number Maya panics about is her own (an example), framed as hers.
- Floey's tip must be true and something the viewer can do today, sourced in the brief.
