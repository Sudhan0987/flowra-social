## 1. The voiced frustration
Formula: A character says [the frustration your viewer has] out loud: 'You [did the work] and still can't [know or get the result].' A second voice answers, captioned.

Viewers hear their own complaint from someone else in the first second, so they stay to find out whether it gets answered. This is the structure behind our Maya and Floey duo reels, which are clearly our best at holding attention. Picture: Frame one: Maya mid-sentence and visibly annoyed, with her line as a one-line caption and the problem object (a chart, a page) beside her. Floey's answer gets its own caption by 1.5 seconds, labelled with her name. Use our own characters, never fake posts or quotes.

For Flowra: "You rewrote the page and still can't tell whether it worked."

## 2. The aftermath first
Formula: Your [page or result] already [took the hit or got the harsh verdict]. Here's [when, where or why] it happened.

When the damage or the verdict comes first, the viewer has to stay for the cause. The road traffic videos open on the injured rider or label a calm ride 'TOTAL LOSS'. The peanut Short names the judge ('THE DEVS') and then tells the viewer how to feel ('THIS IS VERY HURTFUL'). Either way, the outcome lands before any detail. We borrow the outcome-first structure, never the subject. This pattern now includes last week's 'brutal report card'. ## 3. The screenshot receipt

People can read an interface they already know in a split second, and one highlighted line tells the eye where to look, so the claim feels true before anyone speaks. A reacting face underneath tells the viewer how to feel about it. A bare dashboard with no reaction gives them nothing. Picture: Top half: the screen, with one line highlighted. Bottom half: a reacting character. A short caption sits above both. ## Avoid
- Slow garden openings: an establishing shot, a logo, or Floey flying in. Our garden and pro reels mostly lose viewers at 2 to 4 seconds, while 9 of 11 Maya and Floey duo reels, which open on the problem said out loud, hold people for 7.3 seconds or more. Put the problem on screen and say it in frame one.
- Corporate slides with a question as the headline. The answer hubs Short (z65X5U7XMIw) opens on 'How do you build answer hubs that rank in Google AI Overviews?' in blue type on a white slide, and the Google AI Mode Short (NTFeqFAIlEs) opens on a grey product slide with a paragraph of body text. Use one large line that makes a claim, not a slide that asks something.
- Copying yes or no headlines, even when the layout works. 'Is Google Liable?' (KO4LHz0Pwa4) and 'Have website traffic?' (HcWARkOFjDg) let the viewer answer and scroll. Keep the pinned bar but turn it into a specific claim about the viewer's site.
- Talking heads with no caption, where the hook is only in the title or on the cover. The Adsterra Short (HcWARkOFjDg) opens on a man mid-gesture with no text, and its only caption ('Wait, you're still here?') comes at the end. Viewers watching muted get nothing in frame one.
- A calm build-up with no label. The motorbike Short (xbWO6DvxZS0) opens on an ordinary ride and puts 'TOTAL LOSS' only on the cover, which nobody sees in the feed. Name the loss in frame one.
- Asking for saves as the hook or the ending. The first reel of ours that asked people to save got none, and across 16 reels we have only 4 saves and 2 shares. It's one reel, so an early signal, but asking hasn't helped.
- Reading a keyword match as topic evidence. This week's top score (AUZAIx0xuyY) and the motorbike crash (xbWO6DvxZS0) matched the word 'traffic', and the taxi Short (FkNbN9o_WgU) is a marketing story about cars. Borrow structure only, never subject.
- Inventing quotes, posts or testimonials. Complaint posts and 'they said' lines work for strangers, but Flowra never invents customers or user posts. Have Maya or Floey voice the frustration, and keep 'they said' to common advice, never a named person.
- Greetings and self-introductions ('Hi, I'm Floey'). Earlier 'Hi!' openings scored among the lowest, and greetings break the house rules.
- A mascot who only appears on the cover. Floey or Maya has to be in frame one, reacting to the problem.
- Unedited screen recordings as the opening. A plain analytics or tool screen with no caption, face or highlight gives the viewer nothing to look at. The AI Overview Shorts that work highlight one line and put a reaction under it.
- Hype numbers and invented counters ('Rank and scale faster', made-up before and after figures). A step count is fine only when Flowra's process really has that many steps.
- Calling the before and after comparison a split test or A/B test, or promising indexing. Flowra compares before and after with a 14 day settling window, asks Google to recrawl, and never guarantees reindexing.
- Walls of text and stat collages. Nobody can read that much in the first seconds. Use one line, at most two.
- Long tutorial setups (the 62 to 179 second videos). They promise lessons that don't fit a 25 to 45 second reel. Open on the single loss, not the course outline.
- Treating the ranking as exact. There are still no spoken openings, and several top scores come from channels with 14 to 120 subscribers (some likely paid ads). The steadiest evidence comes from the 1,870 to 263,000 subscriber channels and from our own 16 reels.
