# Hook library (2026-10-10)

Studied 14 top Shorts; saw the real opening of 0, the title and cover of the rest.

This week is weak evidence. None of the 14 openings were captured, so we only have titles and covers, and the top two scorers are Hindi AI video tutorials, not SEO. Even so, the covers that broke out do the same three things: they name a frustration the viewer already has, they promise it is easier than they think, and they put a reacting face or character next to one big line of text. The SEO-specific ones that travelled either make a flat claim against common belief or show real proof on screen (a Google result, a chart).

## 7. The chart in frame one
Formula: Your [traffic chart] shows [the movement]. It won't show you [the cause].

A line moving sharply up or down is easy to read in a split second, and a shocked face beside it tells the viewer it matters. Flowra should only show the chart and the gap, never a made-up result. Picture: A dashboard line with one sharp turn, labelled, with a reacting face or character. For Floey: the garden's single total line dips, then she zooms down to the one flower that caused it.

## Avoid
- A slow start before the problem (logo, garden establishing shot, Floey flying in). Our own early signal is that viewers leave in the first few seconds and watch only 2 to 10 percent, so the problem has to be on screen and spoken in frame one.
- Greetings and self-introductions ('Hi, I'm Floey'). They break the house rules and waste the 2 seconds a hook has to land.
- Yes or no questions copied from strangers' titles ('Can your website handle the traffic?'). The viewer can answer 'yes' and scroll away. Restate them as a specific claim about the viewer's site.
- Hype numbers like 'Drive millions' or 'Rank and scale faster'. They are made-up results, break our rules on statistics, and promise outcomes Flowra can't back.
- Walls of thumbnail text (several badges, checklists, 'FULL GUIDE'). These covers are thumbnails, not real opening frames, and a 25 to 45 second animation can't be read that fast. Use one line, at most two.
- Long tutorial setups (the 114 to 152 second videos). Their covers promise step-by-step lessons, which doesn't suit Flowra's short reels. Open on the single loss, not the course outline.
- Weighting this week's patterns heavily. Every example is cover only, and the two top scorers are off-topic Hindi AI video tutorials, so treat these as structures to test, not proven rules.
