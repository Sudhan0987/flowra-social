## 1. The voiced frustration
Formula: A character says [the frustration your viewer has] out loud: 'You [did the work] and still can't [know or get the result].' A second voice answers, captioned.

Viewers hear their own complaint come from someone else in the first second, so they stay to see if it gets answered. This is how our Maya and Floey duo reels open, and they are clearly our best at getting seen and holding attention. Picture: Frame one: Maya mid-sentence and visibly annoyed. Her line is a one-line caption, and the problem object (a drooping flower, a chart line with no figures) is beside her. By 1.5 seconds Floey answers in her own caption, labelled with her name. Use our own characters, never fake posts or quotes, and don't open on a number.

For Flowra: "You rewrote the page and still can't tell whether it worked."
On our reels: Still our strongest and most reliable result: 17 of our 18 Maya and Floey duo reels passed 100 Instagram views, and most held people longer than the garden and pro reels. They still almost never get saved or shared. The five newest reels reached 528 people and got zero saves and zero shares. This pattern wins attention, but we haven't found what earns saves yet.

## Avoid
- Slow garden openings: an establishing shot, a logo, or Floey flying in. Our garden and pro reels mostly lose viewers at 2 to 4 seconds. 17 of our 18 Maya and Floey duo reels open on the problem said out loud, and they passed 100 Instagram views. Put the problem on screen and say it in frame one.
- Opening on a number. Across our 23 reels, hooks that open on a number look weaker. It's our strongest hook signal so far, but still medium at best. Even where strangers open on counters (j1G09YMYfgU), name the loss in words and show a picture.
- Hype promises of traffic arriving by itself. The Phantom Short (lVPQxaqFEEk) opens on a crowned hooded figure, then a neon poster with 'Get more website visitors automatically!' and a 'Download for free' button. It comes from a 220 subscriber channel and is likely paid. Flowra finds and fixes losing pages and measures the change. It never promises traffic, rankings or indexing.
- Corporate slides with a question as the headline. The answer hubs Short (z65X5U7XMIw) opens on 'How do you build answer hubs that rank in Google AI Overviews?' in blue type on a white slide. The Google AI Mode Short (NTFeqFAIlEs) opens on a grey product slide with a paragraph of body text, and its score comes from a 14 subscriber channel. Use one large line that makes a claim, not a slide that asks something.
- Copying yes or no headlines, even when the layout works. 'Is Google Liable?' (KO4LHz0Pwa4) and 'Have website traffic?' (HcWARkOFjDg) let the viewer answer and scroll. Keep the pinned bar, but turn it into a specific claim about the viewer's site.
- Talking heads with no caption, where the hook is only in the title or on the cover. The Adsterra Short (HcWARkOFjDg) opens on a man mid-gesture with no text, and its only caption comes at the end. It still climbed to 52.39, so this is a judgement call, not a proven loss, but muted viewers get nothing in frame one.
- Adding a reaction cam on top of a reposted meme. Earlier, the same 'My bad for asking' screenshot scored better on its own (_Pd72w2dqfc) than with a streamer stacked above it (aEVd7qZEihI). Only add a reaction when it's our own character and it says or shows something new.
- A calm build-up with no label. The motorbike Short (xbWO6DvxZS0) opens on an ordinary ride and puts 'TOTAL LOSS' only in the title and cover, which nobody sees once the video plays. Name the loss in frame one.
- Asking for saves or sends as the hook or the ending. Asking hasn't helped: the five newest reels reached 528 people and got zero saves and zero shares, and the save and share totals across all 23 reels are tiny. It's an early signal.
- Reading a keyword match as topic evidence. The motorbike crash (xbWO6DvxZS0) and the earlier road videos matched the word 'traffic' but are about roads. Borrow structure only, never subject.
- Leading with a big statistic Flowra can't back. The bot traffic Short (ng1-5nX6YAI) puts '53% of internet traffic is now bots' in its title. Flowra never invents or borrows percentages for hooks.
- Inventing quotes, posts or testimonials. Complaint posts and 'they said' lines work for strangers, but Flowra never invents customers or user posts. Have Maya or Floey voice the frustration, and keep 'they said' to common advice, never a named person.
- Greetings and self-introductions ('Hi, I'm Floey'). Earlier 'Hi!' openings scored among the lowest, and greetings break the house rules.
- A mascot who only appears on the cover. Floey or Maya has to be in frame one, reacting to the problem.
- Unedited screen recordings as the opening. A plain analytics or tool screen with no caption, face or highlight gives the viewer nothing to look at. The AI Overview Shorts that work show a query and a single answer, with a one-line caption or a reaction on it.
- Calling the before and after comparison a split test or A/B test, or promising indexing. Flowra compares before and after with a 14 day settling window and asks Google to recrawl, but it never guarantees reindexing.
- Walls of text and stat collages. Nobody can read that much in the first seconds. Use one line, at most two.
- Long tutorial setups (the 75 to 179 second videos). They promise lessons that don't fit a 25 to 45 second reel. Open on the single loss, not the course outline.
- Treating the ranking as exact. There are still no spoken openings. The top scores come from channels with 14 to 220 subscribers, and some are likely paid ads. The steadiest evidence comes from the 1,960 to 277,000 subscriber channels and from our own 23 reels.
