## 1. The voiced frustration
Formula: A character says [the frustration your viewer has] out loud: 'You [did the work] and still can't [know or get the result].' A second voice answers, captioned.

Viewers hear their own complaint from someone else in the first second, so they stay to find out whether it gets answered. This is the structure behind our Maya duo reels, which are now clearly our best at holding attention. Picture: Frame one: Maya mid-sentence and visibly annoyed, with her line as a one-line caption and the problem object (a chart, a page) beside her. The answering voice gets its own caption, labelled with who is speaking, by 1.5 seconds. The new stranger example works the same way: a streamer mid-argument under a caption naming the test, then his AI's reply captioned under a 'Bernard (his AI)' label. Use our own characters, never fake posts or quotes.

For Flowra: "You rewrote the page and still can't tell whether it worked."

## 2. The screenshot receipt
Formula: Your [tool or result you trust] says [the thing, highlighted]. [A character reacts], and here's what it hides.

People can read an interface they already know in a split second, and one highlighted line tells the eye where to look, so the claim is believable before anyone speaks. A reaction face underneath tells the viewer how to feel about it. A bare dashboard with no reaction gives them nothing. Picture: Top half: the screen, with one line highlighted. The other labels its calm ride 'TOTAL LOSS' and puts a shocked face on the cover, so every quiet second becomes suspense. We borrow the structure (outcome first, cause after) and nothing else. ## Avoid
- Slow garden openings: an establishing shot, a logo, or Floey flying in. Our garden reels lose viewers at 2.2 to 4.2 seconds, while the Maya duo reels that open on the problem said out loud hold 4.8 to 9.6. The problem has to be on screen and spoken in frame one.
- A calm build-up with no label. The motorbike Short (xbWO6DvxZS0) opens on an ordinary ride with no caption and puts 'TOTAL LOSS' only on the cover. In the feed nobody sees the cover, so the first seconds give no reason to wait. Name the loss in frame one.
- Talking heads with no caption and the hook only on the cover. The AI product review Short (v3stxqhq6eg) opens on a masked presenter at a mic with no text, while all the promise sits on a crowded cover card. Viewers watching muted get nothing.
- Reading a keyword match as topic evidence. This week's top score (AUZAIx0xuyY), the motorbike crash (xbWO6DvxZS0) and an AI-generated traffic police clip (KYgBVj9rrYc) all matched the word 'traffic'. Borrow structure only, never subject.
- Corporate slide decks. The Google AI Mode Short (NTFeqFAIlEs) opens on a grey product slide with a paragraph of body text. One large line, like the Cludo opening, is the most a slide can carry.
- Inventing quotes, posts or testimonials. The complaint-post format works for strangers, but Flowra never invents customers or user posts. Have Maya or Floey voice the frustration instead.
- Greetings and self-introductions ('Hi, I'm Floey'). Earlier 'Hi!' openings scored among the lowest, and greetings break the house rules.
- A mascot that only appears on the cover. Floey or Maya has to be in frame one, reacting to the problem.
- Unedited screen recordings as the opening. A plain browser, analytics or tool screen with no caption, face or highlight gives the viewer nothing. The AI Overview Shorts that work highlight one line and put a reaction under it.
- Yes or no questions copied from strangers' titles ('Can your website handle the traffic?'). The viewer can answer and scroll away. Restate them as a specific claim about the viewer's site.
- Hype numbers and invented counters ('Rank and scale faster', made-up before and after figures). A step count is fine only if Flowra's process really has that many steps.
- Calling the before and after comparison a split test or A/B test, or promising indexing. Flowra compares before and after with a settling window, asks Google to recrawl, and never guarantees reindexing.
- Walls of text and stat collages. Nobody can read that much in the first seconds. Use one line, at most two.
- Long tutorial setups (the 50 to 179 second videos). They promise lessons that don't fit Flowra's 25 to 45 second reels. Open on the single loss, not the course outline.
- Treating the ranking as exact. There are still no spoken openings, and many top scores come from channels with 0 to 600 subscribers (some likely paid ads). The steadiest evidence comes from the 1,650 to 251,000 subscriber channels and from our own 11 reels.
