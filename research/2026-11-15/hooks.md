## 1. The voiced frustration
Formula: A character says [the frustration your viewer has] out loud: 'You [did the work] and still can't [know or get the result].' A second voice answers, captioned.

Viewers hear their own complaint come from someone else in the first second, so they stay to see whether it gets answered. This is the structure behind our Maya and Floey duo reels, which are clearly our best at getting seen and holding attention. Picture: Frame one: Maya mid-sentence and visibly annoyed, with her line as a one-line caption and the problem object (a chart, a page) beside her. Floey's answer gets its own caption by 1.5 seconds, labelled with her name. Use our own characters, never fake posts or quotes.

For Flowra: "You rewrote the page and still can't tell whether it worked."

## Avoid
- Slow garden openings: an establishing shot, a logo, or Floey flying in. Our garden and pro reels mostly lose viewers at 2 to 4 seconds, while 12 of our 13 Maya and Floey duo reels, which open on the problem said out loud, passed 100 Instagram views and mostly held people for 7 seconds or more. Put the problem on screen and say it in frame one.
- Corporate slides with a question as the headline. The answer hubs Short (z65X5U7XMIw) opens on 'How do you build answer hubs that rank in Google AI Overviews?' in blue type on a white slide. The Google AI Mode Short (NTFeqFAIlEs) opens on a grey product slide with a paragraph of body text, and its score comes from a 14 subscriber channel. Use one large line that makes a claim, not a slide that asks something.
- Copying yes or no headlines, even when the layout works. 'Is Google Liable?' (KO4LHz0Pwa4) and 'Have website traffic?' (HcWARkOFjDg) let the viewer answer and scroll. Keep the pinned bar but turn it into a specific claim about the viewer's site.
- Talking heads with no caption, where the hook is only in the title or on the cover. The Adsterra Short (HcWARkOFjDg) opens on a man mid-gesture with no text, and its only caption ('Wait, you're still here?') comes at the end. It still climbed this week, so this is a judgement call, not a proven loss, but muted viewers get nothing in frame one.
- Adding a reaction cam on top of a reposted meme. The same 'My bad for asking' screenshot scored 7.61 on its own (_Pd72w2dqfc) and 4.5 with a streamer stacked above it (aEVd7qZEihI). The face added nothing. Only add a reaction when it is our own character and it says or shows something new.
- A calm build-up with no label. The motorbike Short (xbWO6DvxZS0) opens on an ordinary ride and puts 'TOTAL LOSS' only on the cover, which nobody sees in the feed. Name the loss in frame one.
- Asking for saves or sends as the hook or the ending. The first save-ask and send-ask reels have produced nothing yet, and across 18 reels we have only 5 saves and 2 shares. It's an early signal, but asking hasn't helped.
- Reading a keyword match as topic evidence. The motorbike crash (xbWO6DvxZS0), the helmet-cam vlog (AUZAIx0xuyY) and the traffic wave explainer (kS3YCq9XsSo) matched the word 'traffic' but are about roads. Borrow structure only, never subject.
- Leading with a big statistic Flowra can't back. The bot traffic Short (ng1-5nX6YAI) puts '53% of internet traffic is now bots' in its title. Flowra never invents or borrows percentages for hooks, and should name the loss in words, not numbers.
- Inventing quotes, posts or testimonials. Complaint posts and 'they said' lines work for strangers, but Flowra never invents customers or user posts. Have Maya or Floey voice the frustration, and keep 'they said' to common advice, never a named person.
- Greetings and self-introductions ('Hi, I'm Floey'). Earlier 'Hi!' openings scored among the lowest, and greetings break the house rules.
- A mascot who only appears on the cover. Floey or Maya has to be in frame one, reacting to the problem.
- Unedited screen recordings as the opening. A plain analytics or tool screen with no caption, face or highlight gives the viewer nothing to look at. The AI Overview Shorts that work show a query and a single answer or highlighted line, with a caption or reaction under it.
- Hype numbers and invented counters ('Rank and scale faster', made-up before and after figures). A step count is fine only when Flowra's process really has that many steps.
- Calling the before and after comparison a split test or A/B test, or promising indexing. Flowra compares before and after with a 14 day settling window and asks Google to recrawl, but it never guarantees reindexing.
- Walls of text and stat collages. Nobody can read that much in the first seconds. Use one line, at most two.
- Long tutorial setups (the 78 to 179 second videos). They promise lessons that don't fit a 25 to 45 second reel. Open on the single loss, not the course outline.
- Treating the ranking as exact. There are still no spoken openings, and several top scores come from channels with 14 to 158 subscribers (some are likely paid ads). The steadiest evidence comes from the 1,960 to 269,000 subscriber channels and from our own 18 reels.
