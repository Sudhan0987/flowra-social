## Script

Voice: Floey, steady pace (Tutorial 1's voice). Screens: animated Search Console mockups in Tutorial 1's style, labelled "Example data". Never a real customer's numbers. Text in [brackets] is what the screen shows.

### 0:00 · Hook (0:30)

[An AI Overview on a results page, with a small link to example-blog.com inside it. Floey points at the link.]

Your page might be showing up inside Google's AI answers right now, and until recently you had no way to know.

Google added a report to Search Console that counts how often your pages appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Since the 31st of August 2026, every site has it. In the next five minutes: where it is, how to read it, and the three things it won't tell you.

### 0:30 · Where the report is (0:35)

[Search Console sidebar: Performance expands; a "Generative AI" item under it is highlighted. The chart opens.]

Open Search Console and pick your site. Under Performance, look for the generative AI report for Search. Discover has its own, separate one.

[An empty state card: "Not enough data".]

If it's not there, or it says there isn't enough data, that usually means your site hasn't had enough impressions in AI features yet. It is not a penalty. Small sites often see this first.

### 1:05 · What an impression means here (0:45)

[The chart with one big number: Impressions. A small definition card.]

The report has one metric: impressions. An impression is a time a link to your site was shown in an AI feature on Google Search.

[An AI Overview with a "Show more" fold; a link below the fold lights up only after it opens.]

Two details matter. If your link sits behind a "show more" fold, it only counts when someone opens it. And if your site appears several times in one answer, the chart counts it once.

[An AI Mode chat with a follow-up question.]

In AI Mode, a follow-up question counts as a new search. So one conversation can add more than one impression.

### 1:50 · The four tabs (1:10)

[The table under the chart, tab by tab.]

Under the chart there are four tabs.

Pages shows which of your URLs were shown in AI answers. This is the one to start with. Sort by impressions and you have a list of the pages Google's AI already leans on.

Countries shows where the searches came from. Dates lets you look by day, week or month. And Devices splits desktop, mobile and tablet.

[Filter bar: Search type, Web text-based or Web multimodal; then a URL filter.]

At the top you can filter by search type. Text-based is the normal search box. Multimodal is image search, including Google Lens and Circle to Search. You can also filter to one URL, to follow a single page over time.

[Export button, then a sheet.]

And you can export the chart and table. Anything shown as a tilde or a dash comes out as zero.

### 3:00 · What it does not show (0:50)

[Three cards cross out one by one.]

Now the limits, because they change how you read it.

One: no clicks. It tells you how often you were shown, not how often anyone came to your site.

Two: no queries. You see pages, countries, dates and devices, but not which searches triggered the answer.

Three: AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted together. You can't split one from the other.

[The newest days on the chart drawn as a dotted line.]

Also, the newest days are preliminary. On the chart they're dotted, and they can still change.

### 3:50 · A monthly check you can run (1:00)

[The Pages tab exported to a sheet; a second column from the regular Performance report.]

Here is a simple check for once a month.

Export the Pages tab for the last 28 days. Then open the regular Performance report, set the same dates, and export the Pages tab there too. Put the two side by side.

[Three highlighted rows, one per outcome.]

A page with plenty of AI impressions and strong clicks in Performance is doing its job. Keep it accurate and up to date.

A page with AI impressions but falling clicks is being shown, but people may get their answer without visiting. Give them a reason to click: a tool, a template, a worked example, something the summary can't carry.

And a page that matters to you but never appears in the AI report is worth a closer look. Check what does get shown for that topic, and whether your page answers the question as clearly.

### 4:50 · Close: impressions are not visits (0:40)

[A two-column card: "Shown in AI answers" (Search Console) vs "Visits from AI assistants" (GA4).]

The report tells you that you were shown. It doesn't tell you who came. For that you need visits.
