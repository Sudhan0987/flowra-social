# Wait 14 days before judging a page update

14 days. That is the settling window we leave between updating a page and judging whether the update worked.

When a team rewrites a page that has been losing clicks, it is tempting to open Search Console a few days later and call it. We think that call is too early, and it can be wrong in either direction.

This is the check we run, in order.

1. Tag the change with its date. Note the page, the sections you rewrote and the parts you left alone.

2. Ignore the first 14 days. The page has to be recrawled first, and asking Google for a recrawl does not guarantee one.

3. Compare the period before the change with the period after it, leaving the settling window out.

4. Adjust for the whole site. If every page gained over the same weeks, your rewrite did not earn all of that gain.

5. Report it as a before-and-after comparison. It is not a split test, so describe the result as a likely effect.

Step 4 is the one we would least want to lose. Without it, a client report can give a rewrite credit for a lift the whole site got that month, and the next report has to walk that back.

We built this check into Flowra's change measurement, but a Search Console export and a spreadsheet can do the same job.

How long do you wait after a content update before you report on it to a client or your team?
