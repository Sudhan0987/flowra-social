# The zero-click tail at the bottom of Search Console

7,249 impressions and 0 clicks came from one question, written 389 different ways.

A poster in r/TechSEO found that cluster at the very bottom of their Search Console queries. The wordings read like searches an AI assistant writes, and their conclusion was that some of them weren't typed by a person.

They now run the check every week:

1. Open the Performance report and go to the Queries tab.
2. Sort by clicks and scroll to the bottom.
3. Read through the zero-click long tail.
4. Look for one question repeated in many wordings.

Step 4 is the slow one. The sort puts every zero-click row together, but it doesn't group them by meaning, so you have to read the rows yourself.

On screen that's a long run of 0 in the Clicks column, with an impression count still sitting next to each one.

If your impressions went up and your clicks didn't move, this tail deserves a look before anyone calls it growth. If you report to a client or a boss, that is where the stakes sit: a rising impressions line can include searches that were never going to send a visit.

Search Console doesn't label who or what typed a query, so this is a pattern to watch rather than proof. We'd log a cluster like that as its own line in the audit and keep it apart from the queries real readers click on.

Then the report carries them as two separate lines. Whoever reads it can see what grew and decide how much of it counts.

We wouldn't strip those impressions out of the totals, either. Hiding them would be a reporting problem of a different kind.

We think the split is worth more than a tidy chart, even when the tidy chart looks better in a monthly review.

What does the zero-click tail on your biggest site look like right now?
