# AI Overviews suits dismissed: check your decaying pages

41 pages, and nothing in them makes Google change AI Overviews.

That is how long Judge Amit Mehta's opinion ran when he dismissed the Chegg and Penske Media antitrust suits over Google's AI Overviews on September 30.

Reuters reported it on October 1 and it has been going around Hacker News since.

Three details from the ruling are worth knowing if you run SEO for a site or for clients:

→ 5 claim types failed, tying and monopoly maintenance among them
→ Penske's case was dismissed without prejudice, in an order that is final and appealable
→ "But an expectation is not an agreement," the judge wrote

Penske can appeal. But a dismissal orders Google to change nothing, so we would plan as if AI Overviews are here for years.

We would not hold any content decisions for the appeal. What is still up to you is knowing which of your pages are losing clicks.

**The decay check we would run this week:**

1. In Search Console, compare the last 3 months with the same 3 months a year earlier.
2. Sort pages by clicks lost, biggest drop at the top.
3. Next to each page, note whether impressions held or fell.
4. Split the list in two on that column.

Pages where impressions held and clicks fell still rank but get fewer visits. Rework those first, before you write anything new.

Pages where impressions fell as well are a different problem, because Google is showing them less often. They can wait until the first group is done.

Write down the date you rework each page. Run the same comparison again next quarter and you will see whether the clicks came back.
