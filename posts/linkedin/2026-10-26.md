# The $0 content audit stack and the four verdicts

$0 is what a content audit stack costs for a site under 500 URLs. Past that, the first bill is a Screaming Frog licence at $279 a year.

A content audit has three jobs: list every URL, see how each one performs, decide what to do with it. No single tool does all three well, so we price the stack.

The free version, line by line:

Screaming Frog free: $0, up to 500 URLs crawled
Ahrefs Webmaster Tools: $0, up to 5,000 pages a month on a verified site
Google Search Console: $0
GA4: $0

That ledger covers the first two jobs. If your site is under 500 URLs, you can start this week without asking anyone for budget.

The third job costs nothing either, and it is the one that gets skipped. One sheet, one row per URL, crawl and performance data side by side, a verdict in the last column.

Keep: clicks steady or growing, content accurate.
Update: clicks or impressions falling, topic still relevant.
Merge: two pages getting impressions for the same queries.
Remove: 12 months with no clicks or impressions and no backlinks, and even then only if nobody can give a business reason to keep the page.

Fill in the verdict for every row before anyone touches a page, so the whole team can argue with the sheet first.

Deleting on traffic alone is the mistake that is hard to undo. AI can cluster thousands of titles, but which pages matter to your business is still your call. If you audit for agency clients, walk them through that column, because they are the ones who know why a page with no traffic still exists.

After the changes, wait at least two weeks and compare equal periods before and after. Keep the before numbers in the same sheet, next to each verdict, so the after check runs on the same rows.

Which verdict do your audits get wrong most often, merge or remove?
