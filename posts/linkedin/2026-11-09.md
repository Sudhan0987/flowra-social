# Three sections of a comparison post that go stale first

About 10% of visitors to PostHog's head-to-head comparison pages signed up in FY2025. Their other blog posts converted at about 2.5%.

Their guide on keeping those pages fresh got traction on Hacker News, and one line from it stuck with us: the article you wrote in March is outdated in June.

People read comparison pages when they are close to picking a tool. A wrong price or a wrong checkmark there gets seen by exactly the readers you were hoping to convert.

The decay is not spread evenly. Three sections go first.

1. **Pricing and plans.** PostHog keeps 14 head-to-head pages, each with pricing that changes every quarter. Check each price and which plan each feature sits on. Open each competitor's live pricing page next to yours and read them side by side, line by line.

2. **The feature table.** An Amplitude employee sent PostHog a PR fixing four features marked as missing that Amplitude actually has. Every row is a judgment call, and judgments age. Read your table the way the competitor would. Any row they could dispute is a row to recheck.

3. **Your own column.** In their words, a very embarrassing way to get a comparison wrong is understating yourself. PostHog shipped a full APM suite and updated the pages to say so. When your team ships something, the comparison pages are easy to forget, so put them on the release checklist and make every launch include an edit.

Update those three before you think about rewriting the whole page. A full rewrite feels productive, but the decay sits in a few sections, and fixing them is a smaller job you will actually finish.

Save them as a quarterly checklist, since that is how often the prices move. If you look after comparison pages for agency clients, one shared list across every client's pages means the check no longer depends on whoever wrote the page.

Which section on your comparison pages went stale last?
