# AI agrees on humans, not on who to recommend

24 of 99 local picks overlapped when ChatGPT and Perplexity were asked who to hire.

They named the same #1 business once in 20 searches.

That comes from a Clicks Dynasty test on 26 September: 20 searches like "best plumber in Fresno, CA" across 5 California cities and 4 industries. Same prompts, same session, a new chat each time. The only variable was the tool.

The author thinks the two tools read different parts of the web. It was one run per prompt, so treat the 24% as directional.

Set that next to a different test. ModelCompare asked 13 AI models for their most controversial opinion of people, three times, and 32 of 38 answers gave the same "controversial" take on humans. In GPT-6.1 Sol's words: "Humans are more loyal to belonging than to truth."

So the models agree on a big abstract question and split on who should fix a sink in Fresno.

That is the part that worries us for client reporting. A screenshot of one ChatGPT answer says "you are recommended," and the same question in Perplexity could name someone else.

Type "best plumber in Fresno, CA" into both tools and, in 19 of those 20 searches, you would be looking at two different names in the #1 spot.

Before a ChatGPT screenshot goes into a client report, we would want:
- the same prompt run in Perplexity, in a fresh chat
- a note of who sits at #1, and whether the client is in the list at all
- more than one run, because one run per prompt is only directional
- a look at whether any visits actually arrived from either tool

What we trust more are the visits that actually arrive. AI referrals show up in GA4 as referrers, which is how Flowra counts them. Referral counting still cannot see an answer that never sent a click.

How are you reporting AI visibility to clients right now: screenshots, referral visits, or something else?
