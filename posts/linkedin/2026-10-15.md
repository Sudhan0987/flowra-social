# Map queries to sections before cutting

Most content refreshes that lose rankings don't fail because of what got added. They fail because of what got cut.

You open an old post, spot a section that looks dated or off topic, and trim it. Then clicks drop, because that section was quietly answering a query that sent steady traffic.

The fix is one small step before you touch the draft:

1. In Search Console, filter to the page and sort its queries by impressions.
2. For each query, search the page in your browser and find the section that answers it.
3. Mark every section as either earning traffic or matching no queries.

Sections that earn traffic get careful edits. Keep the heading wording and the core answer, and update around them. Sections with no queries are where you can cut, merge or rewrite freely.

It turns "this part feels old" into a decision you can back with data.
