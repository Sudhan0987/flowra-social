# Check section queries before cutting dated content

The section that looks most dated in an old blog post can be the one earning its clicks.

In a refresh, the instinct is to trim whatever reads old: a section built around an old year, tool or price. Some of those sections still answer real searches, and cutting them takes that traffic with them.

The fix is a four-step check in Search Console before you delete a single heading. You take the page's own queries and match each one to the section that answers it. Whatever has no queries behind it can go.

Swipe for the check, how long to wait before you judge the result, and what to put back if the page drops.

When you refresh an old post, do you check section-level queries first, or go by how dated it reads?
