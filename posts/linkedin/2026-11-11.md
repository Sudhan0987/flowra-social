# A page that dropped out of Google: where Search Console logs it

500 to 700 Search Console impressions a day, then almost zero, in one day.

A developer posted that in an Ask HN thread this week, asking if Google has started cleaning AI slop out of results. Nobody in the thread had confirmation from Google, so we won't claim a cleanup either.

One reply cited a Graphite sample of Common Crawl: about 5% of new articles were mostly AI-written when ChatGPT launched, and about 50% from late 2024 to May 2026. Their warning was that any filter aimed at that much of the web will catch real sites too.

Our view: rule out your own changes before you blame a filter. You can check those today, with dates attached.

If a page was indexed last month and isn't now, Search Console has a record of it:

1. Open the Pages report and go to "Why pages aren't indexed". Click the row and note the First detected date. Put that date next to the day your impressions fell.

2. Read the reason. A noindex, a 404 or a redirect usually points to a change on your side. If so, check what your team or the client's developers shipped around that date. Crawled - currently not indexed means Google saw the page and left it out.

3. Run URL Inspection on the page. Check the last crawl date, then Test live URL. Save the result with the date, so the next drop has something to compare against.

One limit: the examples table stops at 1,000 rows and isn't guaranteed to list every affected URL, so a big site can hide drops. If you manage a large site or several client sites, don't treat that table as the full list.

Flowra's Index Coverage Monitor records the date a page's index status changes, which gives you a dated history for each page.
