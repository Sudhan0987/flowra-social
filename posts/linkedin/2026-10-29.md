# ChatGPT citations vs real visits: the GA4 landing page check

24 of 99. That is how many of the businesses Perplexity named also showed up in ChatGPT's top five, across 20 local searches run with the same prompt in both tools on 26 September 2026.

Put the other way round, 75 of the 99 Perplexity picks were missing from ChatGPT's top five.

The setup, so you can judge it yourself:

- Prompt: "best {industry} in {city}, CA"
- 5 cities and 4 industries, 20 searches in total
- The same prompt typed into ChatGPT and into Perplexity
- One session, run once

The study is small and ran once, so some of that gap is noise. We would not quote 24 of 99 as a rate for every market, and we would push back on anyone who did.

Still, if a citation can change with the tool, or with whether you are signed in, it is a shaky number to put in a client report.

Think about how that plays out in a monthly report. A line saying the client is "cited by ChatGPT" describes one tool on one day.

The client asks Perplexity the same question and gets a different list. Now your report is arguing with their screen, and you are the one explaining the gap.

That is a conversation worth avoiding before it starts.

The check we would run instead: which landing pages AI assistants actually sent people to in GA4, and what to do with that list.

A visit is a different kind of evidence from a screenshot of an answer. It happened on your site, it sits in your own data and you can pull the same report again next month.

The check has one real limit: referral counting cannot see an answer that never sent a click.

Which page on your site gets the most AI referrals right now?

P.S. Flowra's AI traffic report reads the same GA4 referrers, so it has the same blind spot.
