# Content pruning: clicks measure demand, not value

Sort your pages by clicks and the bottom rows look like dead weight. Many of them aren't.

Clicks measure demand. They don't measure value.

Before you prune, check every low-traffic page for a job that search data won't show you:

Conversion pages. A comparison post with modest views can still bring in leads.
Link earners. Original research, templates and glossaries pick up backlinks quietly.
Support and sales assets. Help guides that get shared by email, not found in Google.
Seasonal pages. A tax guide is quiet most of the year.
New pages. Anything under six months old hasn't had a fair run.
Niche audiences. Pages for specialist roles never get big numbers.

In the guide's worked example, an old launch page has 5 clicks and 12 external links. On clicks alone you'd delete it. Because of the links, it gets a 301 to the closest relevant page instead.

That's why the safest first batch has no deletions at all. Take the 20 pages losing the most clicks, check them for overlap, and make them better.
