# Content pruning: low traffic is not low quality

A comparison post with 30 visits a month and five demo requests is one of the best pages on a site.

Sort a pruning sheet by Search Console clicks and it lands at the bottom, right next to a page with 30 visits and nothing else. Same traffic, same row.

The only variable is what the page does after the click.

Low traffic is not proof of low quality.

Before anything goes on the delete list, check whether it converts, earns links, gets sent out by sales, is seasonal or is under six months old. Age alone is never a reason.

Then ask five questions in order and stop at the first yes:

1. Another page covers the same intent? Merge into the stronger one.
2. Still relevant and still searched? Update it.
3. Needed outside search, by customers, sales or legal? Keep it and noindex it.
4. Has external links or a page a visitor would happily land on instead? 301 it there.
5. None of the above? Delete it and return a 410.

Merge comes first on purpose: overlapping pages are on topics you care about and people search for.

Ship a few dozen pages at most, wait four to six weeks, and log every URL, action, destination and date. If something goes wrong, you know which change caused it and you only have a small set to undo.

What is the lowest-traffic page on your site you would never delete?
