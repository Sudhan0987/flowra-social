# Content audit template: merge before you refresh or delete

A page lost 30% of its clicks year over year. The usual move is to rewrite it. If a page is thin and dead, the usual move is to delete it.

Both judge the page alone, and that is where they go wrong.

Rewrite it, and you may be polishing one of two pages chasing the same keyword. Refreshing one of two competing pages rarely fixes the competition.

Delete it, and the referring domains it earned stop pointing anywhere useful. A weak page with links should almost never be deleted outright.

So our content audit template checks four rules in order. The first match wins:

1. Merge if another page targets the same keyword. Combine into the higher-traffic URL and 301 the other.
2. Review for redirect or removal: under 10 clicks in 12 months, zero conversions, no referring domains, 6+ months old.
3. Refresh if clicks fell 30% or more year over year.
4. Keep everything else.

Merge goes first so the duplicate check runs before anyone rewrites anything.

Run the 30% page from the top through it. If another URL targets the same keyword, it merges into the higher-traffic one and the rewrite never happens.

If it had 10 or more clicks in the last 12 months, rule 2 passes it by. Only then does rule 3 send it for a refresh, since a 30% drop meets the threshold exactly.

The thin, dead page only lands in rule 2 if nothing links to it. If referring domains point at it, it falls through to rule 3 or 4, and nobody deletes it by reflex.

One trap in rule 3: Search Console keeps 16 months of data, and year over year needs 24. Without saved exports, use GA4 organic sessions per landing page in both columns.

The template is a free .xlsx for Excel or Google Sheets, no email needed. Link in the first comment.
