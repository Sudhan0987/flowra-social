# save-ask: Did the rewrite work? Tag, wait 14 days, compare

14 days. That is how long we let a rewritten page settle before we judge it.

Most rewrites never get judged at all. The new version goes live, someone opens Search Console a few days later, clicks look up or down, and the team moves on with a story instead of an answer.

The check we run has three steps. Save it for the next page you touch.

Step 1: Tag the change date.
Write down the day the new version went live, somewhere the whole team can see it. A shared doc or the page's row in your content tracker is enough. Without that date, every chart you look at later is a guess about where "before" ends.

Step 2: Wait out the 14 day settling window.
The first days after publishing are the ones you will most want to read and the ones we trust least. Give the page two weeks before you pull a single number.

Step 3: Compare before and after, adjusted for the whole site.
If the page gained clicks while the whole site gained about the same, the rewrite probably did little. If the page held steady while the site dropped, it may have done a lot.

Treat this as a before-and-after comparison. A split test would need a control page and random assignment, and this has neither, so the result is good enough to make a decision but will not prove the rewrite caused the change.

Skipping it has a cost. You rewrite the next ten pages based on a guess about the first one, and if that guess was wrong, you have repeated the mistake ten times.
