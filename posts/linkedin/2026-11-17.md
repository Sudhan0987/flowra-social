# Client report: fixed pages, not post count

When a client writes "AI writes the posts now, so why pay you," most teams pick one of two answers. Both keep the report about post count.

They cut the fee, or they defend the writing.

18 comments under one r/marketing question this week: has AI pushed marketing service prices down?

Content people in that thread say they feel the pressure. When it reaches a client email, it tends to be one line, the one above.

Cutting the fee feels like the safe answer. It accepts the client's math. If the job is posts and a tool makes posts, the price of a post falls, and the same email comes back next quarter at a lower number.

Defending the writing sounds braver, but it argues inside the same frame. You end up comparing your sentences with a tool's sentences, while the report on the client's desk still counts posts.

The way out is to change what the report counts.

**What to put in place of post count:**
1. The old pages you caught falling in Search Console
2. What you changed on each
3. What happened once the change had 14 days to settle

You have probably watched an old page slip in Search Console, the clicks line bending down week after week, while the monthly report showed another tidy post count.

A post count describes output. Those three lines describe judgment, which is the part a client cannot get by typing a prompt.

The 14 days are there for a reason: judge a change too early and you show the client noise.

The slides go through why both answers leave the report about post count, and how to build each of those three lines.

We built Flowra's client report on the same idea. It is a share link that rebuilds every week and carries your agency's name and logo on Starter and up.
